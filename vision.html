Opportunity 01
01
New Revenue · Platform Extension · Brand Accounts Subscriptions backbone required

Give sponsors a native presence — not just an ad unit.

GameChanger's current advertising product — placements via DICK'S Media on the home screen and pre-stream — serves national brands well. But thousands of regional and local brands are already physically present at youth sports events. They sponsor teams, show up at tournaments, and have genuine relationships with the families GameChanger serves. They currently have no path onto the platform.

Battle Juice sponsors teams. Our logo is on their jerseys. Our rep is at the field with a GoPro. We have a wrapped trailer we bring to events. We would gladly set teams up on GameChanger ourselves just to be part of the digital experience surrounding those kids. Right now there is no path for us to exist on the platform.

A brand account tier — sponsor profiles, videographer permissions tied to sponsored teams, in-app activation posts, targeted reach — is a natural extension of existing infrastructure. It opens a new revenue line from a category of sponsors who currently have no way to participate.

The on-site play: A sponsor streams the game at the event on a screen at their booth. Their brand is the reason families are watching live at the field. That is a brand moment that no other youth sports platform is positioned to create — and it starts from infrastructure GameChanger already has.
02
Hard Problem · COPPA · NIL · Trust Infrastructure New subscription tier required

Youth NIL, done right — by the only platform with the trust to do it.

The youth NIL space is real and growing. LakePoint Sports launched an NIL-inspired program in June 2025 pairing elite youth players with 60+ brand partners. Research already shows that youth athletes spending significant time on NIL content face higher rates of mental health challenges — meaning the design of any such product carries real responsibility.

GameChanger already has COPPA compliance infrastructure, verified family relationships, and the trust of millions of families. No brand-side startup can replicate that. A well-designed youth NIL layer — strict parental consent at every step, no direct monetization of minors, brand matching that benefits the program rather than the individual, production handled outside the athlete — could be the most responsible implementation of this idea that exists.

This is not a simple engineering problem. It is a compliance problem, a product design problem, and a brand trust problem simultaneously. I have spent meaningful time thinking through the architecture and the failure modes. If GameChanger decides to build here, I would want to be part of that conversation.

Why GameChanger wins here: Nobody else has the verified parent-child relationships already in place at scale. The COPPA infrastructure is the moat. Building on top of it is table stakes compared to any competitor trying to build from scratch.
